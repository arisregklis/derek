The Story

In What Could Have Been

Call it a need to evolve; call it vanity. Whatever a man chooses, he will usually focus on what he lacks rather than what he has. A teacher wants to teach his student to look to the present, to what he holds — and not to wander into distant worlds that have little to offer. But the student perceives his life differently. He lives in a system with two suns, in a strange sunset: two sides of the same coin, the dual nature of a person lost in immaterial worlds and the titanic nature of the earth.

In this book we find simple stories about people of today and people searching for themselves in a distant world. In what could have been and wasn't. In a parallel universe where the deepest longing has found its port of call to sail — as if a person had found the way back, a Caryatid waiting patiently to return home.

From a rain-soaked café in Paris to the lyre of Psiloritis, from lost Minoans to the paths of Ephesus, the second book by Derek Robotis (Δωρόνικος Ρομποτής) gathers dozens of short stories that travel the reader, in mind, toward coloured nebulae.

From the Pages

The Red Umbrella

Each city has its own characteristics. The real life of a city is discovered in its streets — there, where the life of ordinary people is not surrounded by the false image that should attract the traveller. So it was in the cafés of Paris that Glaucus discovered the beauty of the city. A brief storm forced him to take refuge in a small café decorated with renaissance paintings, like a small museum offering coffee and a few local delicacies.

What struck him was a red umbrella on an empty table. Its red was striking against the grey landscape the rain had formed. The coffee was served hot and delightful as the atmosphere in the café — but the red umbrella stayed forgotten. As he was paying, a young woman in red boots came in in a hurry, grabbed the umbrella, and ran away.

The days went by and Glaucus returned to the café every day to find the girl with the red umbrella. But in vain — she never appeared. He had found a nest for the rainy days of Paris; he had found a girl in red boots. But he never had the courage to ask her if the umbrella was hers. He would forever be left wondering.
