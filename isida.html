The Story

The Dig at Marathon

Greece leaves its past to its destiny — cutbacks, bureaucracy, an Academy that gets tired just thinking about anything new. But the myth of Isida’s pyramid at Marathon has intrigued Aristides too long, and with his colleague Andromache standing by him with all her powers, the vote against them becomes the beginning, not the end.

What they uncover beneath the soil of Attica is an empty sarcophagus — and a door that does not open onto a burial chamber, but onto another era. A strange presence had already come to Aristides in his sleep the night before the Academy’s decision. Now he learns what it wanted.

On the far side of the door wait a king’s death and a People’s Assembly, the aura of the desert and the waters of Hades, the meeting of Nicaea and the kiss of death itself. To come home, Aristides must resist the call of the siren, heed the Founder’s warning, and carry back a knowledge that will not leave his family untouched — for the story ends with a farewell at Isida’s sarcophagus.

From the Pages

The Visit

Archaeological sites in Greece could be better cared for. It is said that cutbacks should be made; that emphasis should be given to the country’s present day, and the past left to its destiny. Without the past there is no future. Whoever doesn’t possess the knowledge of his heritage is doomed to have limited awareness of oneself.

There has been great interest in the fact that in the area of Marathon there is an archaeological site dedicated to Isida. The myth mentions that Herodes Atticus, after a trip to Egypt, decided that he should build a pyramid in honor of the goddess. It has intrigued me for quite a while now. I have to find the truth.

The night before the decision was made, a strange presence came to me in my sleep.
