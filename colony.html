The Story

Humanity’s Second Home

It begins with one man against everyone: a boy from the mother continent who dreamed of the stars and was laughed at for it. Years later his rockets take off and land standing, enemies and friends climb the same mast, and the launch that follows sets humanity sailing for star ports where planets and moons replace sand and sea.

But when Fabian and the first colonists break ground on the red planet, the drills find what no survey predicted — tunnels never carved by human hands, and beneath them the remains of an ancient civilization older than any question mankind has ever asked. Every answer the colonists dig up splits into new dilemmas: who were they, why did they vanish, and why does their legacy feel so strangely familiar?

And then there is the seed. A box of “seeds of the gods” from an old woman in a grocery store near Pompeii, a bitter fruit the old stories called ambrosia — and the one small boy, little Harry, who eats it and shows new abilities every week. Watch him closely at the story’s end: he disappears in a flash of light, with a stranger in a space suit.

From the Pages

The Visionary

From the dawn of his history, man has wondered where he has come from. Perhaps this has always been the age-old question — this primal feeling from the moment we realized we existed. The beginning of the journey, of knowledge itself. A point that stood as a springboard to begin, as a species, the journey to the stars.

As a young boy he had dreamed of reaching the stars, and his dream was to get up there at any cost. His success was not a road full of rose petals. There were times when everyone mocked him. It was just him against everyone. He felt that only the infinite universe understood him.

A hopeless effort to achieve the impossible: to take off a rocket and then land it vertically. And yet he did it. After that moment, nothing stood in his way. The mast had set sail for roads unknown — star ports where instead of sand and sea they would be joined by planets and moons.
