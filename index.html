ArchaeologyGreek MythTime Travel

Archaeologist Aristides has heard the myth all his life: that Herodes Atticus, returning from Egypt, raised a pyramid at Marathon in honor of the goddess Isida. When the Academy votes down the dig, he and his colleague Andromache go anyway — and beneath the soil of Attica they find an empty sarcophagus, and a door that does not open onto a chamber, but onto another era.

From the People's Assembly to the waters of Hades, from the kiss of death to the song of the siren, Aristides must survive a world that remembers the gods — and find his way back to his own time.

Four books, one thread: humanity reaching past its limits — up to the stars, down into the earth, back through time — and finding that something was always reaching back.

The Author

Between Marathon and Mars

Derek Robotis writes at the crossroads where Greek myth meets deep space. His stories begin in familiar places — a school attic, an archaeologist's office in Athens, a cramped detective agency — and end among ancient civilizations, talking stars, and doors through time.

Whether he's following a boy's telescope to the tree of life, digging beneath Marathon for the goddess Isida, or walking the smog-choked streets of Pythos with a detective and his symbiont, one conviction runs through every page: without the past there is no future — and the future is written in the stars.

Excerpt · The Tree

The Attic Window

Harry at school is one of those kids who spends a lot of time alone. He divides his time between his lessons and the small telescope his father had bought him — probably the best present they had ever given him. Looking up at the stars, he felt like he belonged to another world, far from Earth; a feeling inexplicably familiar.

In the evenings, when the sky was clearer, he spent many hours looking at the constellations — Orion, Sagittarius. The small window in the attic was the bridge to the infinite universe, where he gathered his thoughts and dreams which found a foothold in another cosmic dimension. Often he would fall asleep on the window ledge while gazing at the stars.

He saw a tree with green leaves. Not only did he discover a new planet — he discovered life on it. A tree, nothing else. But it was the tree of life on another heavenly body. Life is pervasive in the universe. What mysteries did it hide?

From The Tree — Derek Robotis

Excerpt · The Second Colony

The Visionary

From the dawn of his history, man has wondered where he has come from. Perhaps this has always been the age-old question — this primal feeling from the moment we realized we existed. The beginning of the journey, of knowledge itself. A point that stood as a springboard to begin, as a species, the journey to the stars.

As a young boy he had dreamed of reaching the stars, and his dream was to get up there at any cost. His success was not a road full of rose petals. There were times when everyone mocked him. It was just him against everyone. He felt that only the infinite universe understood him.

A hopeless effort to achieve the impossible: to take off a rocket and then land it vertically. And yet he did it. After that moment, nothing stood in his way. The mast had set sail for roads unknown — star ports where instead of sand and sea they would be joined by planets and moons.

From The Second Colony — Derek Robotis

Excerpt · Shadows and Deceit

The Streets of Pythos

For some reason, the city was unusually gloomy. The pollution was at its highest — you could feel it in the air that you breathed. The F.T.L. drives had spawned suddenly, causing a migration never seen in the galaxy before. Pythos, the planet we are on, had over ten races living on it.

My symbiont, during my sleep, chooses to show me images of the worlds it has visited. In his world he was somewhat a detective too. It's weird to have all these memories and not know how they can assist me.

As soon as we dropped out of F.T.L., the station appeared in all its might — millions of tons spinning to create artificial gravity, a gateway to the galaxy. You will find merchants, people trying to cut a deal for a better life, and people like me: trying to solve a case and find the elusive truth. I know that truth is a matter of perspective.

From Shadows and Deceit — Derek Robotis

Excerpt · The Pyramid of Isida

The Visit

Archaeological sites in Greece could be better cared for. It is said that cutbacks should be made; that emphasis should be given to the country's present day, and the past left to its destiny. Without the past there is no future. Whoever doesn't possess the knowledge of his heritage is doomed to have limited awareness of oneself.

There has been great interest in the fact that in the area of Marathon there is an archaeological site dedicated to Isida. The myth mentions that Herodes Atticus, after a trip to Egypt, decided that he should build a pyramid in honor of the goddess. It has intrigued me for quite a while now. I have to find the truth.

The night before the decision was made, a strange presence came to me in my sleep.

From The Pyramid of Isida — Derek Robotis
